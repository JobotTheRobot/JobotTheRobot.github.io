# Portfolio Content Guide

This guide identifies the content and photos that will make the portfolio easy to understand and specific without inventing details.

## General writing approach

- Lead with the engineering problem, role, and result.
- Use short sections rather than large blocks of resume text.
- Include your role clearly for team projects.
- Describe tools and decisions only when they are accurate.
- Prefer concrete outcomes, measurements, test results, and lessons learned.
- Keep older or unfinished work clearly labeled.
- Use the first person only where it helps explain your decisions.

## Homepage

### Engineering introduction

Write 2 to 3 sentences covering:

- Who you are and what kind of engineering you do.
- The systems you work across.
- What you enjoy building or solving.
- Where you are based.

Suggested frame:

`I am an electrical and computer engineering student working across [areas]. My work includes [types of systems or problems].`

### What I do

Use a short paragraph and add 4 to 6 topics. Good topics include:

- Embedded firmware
- BLDC motor control
- PCB design
- Motion control
- Robotics integration
- Hardware debugging
- Mechanical design

### Featured projects

Feature the BLDC Robot Joint, MSP430 Development Board, and ECE 306 Robot Car.

For each card, write one short description answering:

- What is the project?
- What system did you build or improve?
- Why does it matter?

Use about 20 to 35 words per card.

### Currently

Use 2 short paragraphs:

- What you are working on now.
- A concise summary of CN-Seamless and how your current work fits into it.

Avoid repeating the About page in full.

## Custom BLDC Robot Joint

### Status

Keep: In progress.

### Short overview

Write 2 to 3 sentences covering:

- The motor, control, electronics, and mechanical areas involved.
- Whether the project is targeting a robot joint or a motor-control platform.

### Photo needs

Useful photos:

- Whole assembly view.
- Exploded view of motor, gearbox, encoder, PCB, and mounting components.
- Close-up of the custom PCB.
- Close-up of firmware debugging or encoder feedback.
- Test bench showing the joint being controlled.
- CAD rendering beside the physical assembly.

### Technical details

Add only verified information:

- Microcontroller.
- Gate driver or inverter.
- Encoder or sensor interfaces.
- Control modes implemented.
- Communication interfaces.
- PCB tools and board purpose.
- Gearbox or transmission design.
- Firmware structure.

### Design decisions

Explain 2 to 4 decisions, such as:

- Why a particular MCU or architecture was selected.
- How the control system is organized.
- Why certain sensors or feedback devices were chosen.
- Why the mechanical transmission was structured a particular way.
- How the PCB and mechanical assembly are divided.

### Challenges and results

Include any measured or observed outcomes:

- What failed or required redesign.
- What was difficult to tune or debug.
- What testing proved or disproved.
- Remaining milestones.

No invented specifications or performance claims.
